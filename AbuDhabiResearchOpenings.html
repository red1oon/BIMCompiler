Three research openings in Abu Dhabi infrastructure

Each one meets a stated Abu Dhabi priority and builds on a working open-source platform that already runs whole construction models in a browser tab. Each could be a conference paper first and a journal paper after.

01 · Delivery

A construction schedule worked out from the model itself

Mainstream 4D tools link a model to a schedule someone has already written. Our platform proposes a build sequence straight from the model, and an automatic check confirms each element goes up only after what supports it. That works even on poor data, such as road and bridge files where every object is an unlabelled generic proxy.

Fits ADPIC × ASPIRE's aim to use digital science to optimise infrastructure design, improve operational efficiency.[1] Automatic 4D from IFC already appears in the literature[2], so the new part is working from poor data and checking the result.

02 · Carbon

Embodied carbon straight from the model's quantities

The platform already takes quantities off the model. Multiplying them by an open carbon dataset gives an embodied-carbon estimate per element, per stretch of road and per construction phase. Design options can then be compared on carbon while they are still drawings.

Fits ASPIRE's focus on low-carbon alternatives[1] and the building-materials category of Abu Dhabi's Estidama Pearl Rating System[3]. Open factors are available in the ICE database v4.1[4], and CIBSE launched TM65 UAE, a UAE-specific embodied-carbon method, in January 2025.[9]

03 · Operations

From handover model to asset records

Abu Dhabi's infrastructure BIM guideline says project information must be fed into ADM asset management systems.[5] The platform already turns model elements into structured business records (products, project phases, tasks). The research question is how much of a handover model reaches those records intact.

A UAE study of 14 practitioners found asset management still relies on paper-based systems, with data loss and information gaps.[6] DMT is building a city-wide digital twin.[7]

What exists today: an open-source (MIT) platform that imports IFC, renders, schedules, takes off quantities and checks clashes in one browser tab with no server. It is measured on a 10,413-element road and bridge model.[8] Not yet tested on an IFC 4.3 infrastructure file, which would be a good first joint step.
