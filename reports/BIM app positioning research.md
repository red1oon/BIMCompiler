# Becoming a serious BIM app without hype

**Bottom line.** The notes support one narrow, defensible position: a free, zero-install, local-first tool that chains an IFC model to schedule, quantities and cost in one tab, with reproducible results. They do not support broad claims of novelty or of strong small-firm demand. The viewing job is saturated by free tools, and free or cheap 4D/5D tools already exist: Bonsai, OpenConstructionERP and BEXEL (details below). The notes found no tool that is both browser-only, no-server and explicitly no-AI while also covering 4D and cost, but that search was limited in effort **(inference)**. The "No AI" angle is differentiated because every major vendor is selling AI, but the wording is the project's biggest self-inflicted risk, because the code was written with AI assistance. Credibility will come from published, checkable evidence (validator reports, a test-file pass table, a stated size ceiling), not from badges or the paper date. Solo open-source BIM projects historically plateau at roughly one or two part-time salaries, so growth plans should assume slow and small. Ten prioritized actions are at the end.

## Who needs it: cost-blocked and "is BIM relevant to me?" users, but the evidence is thin

The best-documented barrier for small firms is cost plus lack of client demand. The NBS 2020 report names lack of client demand as the largest barrier, with smaller practices saying BIM is "not relevant for their projects" ([NBS 2020](https://www.thenbs.com/knowledge/national-bim-report-2020)). A single Revit subscription is quoted at about **$3,005 per year** by a third-party aggregator, and Navisworks Manage (clash plus TimeLiner 4D) at about **$2,835 per year** ([myarchitectai](https://www.myarchitectai.com/blog/revit-pricing); [BIM Tools Hub](https://bimtoolshub.com/autodesk-navisworks)). Aggregator prices, not verified at vendor source. Bentley Synchro 4D is quoted at roughly $4,280-$4,980 per year through resellers ([Virtuosity](https://in.virtuosity.com/synchro-4d)). A user who needed author, 4D/clash and a construction system would need several such licences **(inference)**.

Segments with some evidence: small contractors and subcontractors, who mostly use BIM for 3D visualisation and lack skills ([DiVA 4D thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1229877/FULLTEXT01.pdf), search-summary); firms in developing markets citing hardware and software cost (India, Nigeria; [T&F 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15578771.2024.2320108), search-summary); and quantity surveyors, where an OSArch thread says Bonsai has little visibility in searches for QS tools and users want a live cost dashboard in design ([OSArch 2503](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/2503/blender-blenderbim-bonsai-is-not-on-google-search-for-best-bim-tools-for-quantity-surveyors)). That thread is the closest external statement of demand for the "live edit-cost projection" idea, and it is one forum discussion. For 4D, the one questionnaire found (141 respondents, 4D for site safety) rated training cost and implementation time as top barriers and did not analyse firm size ([Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2018.00086/full)).

Jobs most plausibly served, all **(inference)**: a rough 4D programme and quantity check from a client IFC for a small contractor; rate-based take-off for a QS without a licence; a "kick-start" estimate where the owner's stated margin of error is acceptable. Self-builders, students and FM owners have no direct pain-point evidence in the notes. The researchers found no survey of small-firm willingness to adopt open-source or browser BIM, no sourced demand for ERP linkage, and no sourced demand for offline use. Privacy is plausible for confidential work, but only generic vendor blogs were found.

## Where it differs, and where it does not

The real difference is packaging. Incumbents sell separate products per job, with construction suites priced opaquely (Procore estimated at $15k-$80k per year for small to mid contractors by third-party blogs; [scanmanifold](https://www.scanmanifold.com/blog-posts/procore-pricing-2026-contractors)). Free viewers are plentiful (BIMcollab Zoom, Dalux, BIM Vision, usBIM, Autodesk Viewer) and cover viewing, sections, measure and issues ([Revizto](https://revizto.com/resources/blog/top-10-bim-viewers)). Clash detection typically sits in paid tiers, for example BIMcollab's paid tier ([BIMcollab](https://www.bimcollab.com/en/go/free-ifc-viewer/)). Bimlyte is free, local-first and states "no AI", but has no automated clash, 4D or cost ([Bimlyte](https://www.bimlyte.com/)). The notes found no free, browser-only, server-less product combining viewer, 4D, clash, measure, cost and an ERP-style operation log; the researchers state "none found" is a limited-effort claim (about 8 queries), not proof of absence.

Where it does not differ is the more important list. **Bonsai** (free, open source) already offers IFC authoring, cost planning, construction scheduling and a web interface for Gantt charts and cost plans ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2024/09/01/bonsai-previously-blenderbim-add-on-v0-8-0-adds-blender-4-2-and-much-more/)). **OpenConstructionERP** (AGPL-3.0, 885 stars) lists BOQ, BIM take-off, 4D, 5D, clash and offline capability, but runs a Python/Postgres server stack and supports LLM-based estimation ([GitHub](https://github.com/datadrivenconstruction/OpenConstructionERP)). **BEXEL Manager** offers 3D/4D/5D/6D, clash and QTO from about EUR 480 per year ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/185992/BEXEL-Manager/)), and Navisworks already has TimeLiner and Clash Detective. The notes' surviving differentiators are (a) an explicit deterministic, no-AI stance combined with 4D/cost, (b) one tab, no server, and (c) one operation log feeding both BIM and ERP **(inference)**. The notes could not verify feature depth of OpenConstructionERP, Bonsai or That Open beyond their own pages.

Browser limits are real and should be stated by the project itself. Browser viewers are reported workable to roughly 300 MB on a typical desktop (vendor blog; [Revizto](https://revizto.com/resources/blog/top-10-bim-viewers)). web-ifc is bounded by the 32-bit WASM memory ceiling, and a ~2.5 GB hospital IFC crashes the importer ([ThatOpen issue #258](https://github.com/ThatOpen/engine_fragment/issues/258)). Revit imports IFC4 only as a link, so round-trip into Revit is lossy by design ([Autodesk help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2025/ENU/Revit-DocumentPresent/files/GUID-6708CFD6-0AD7-461F-ADE8-6527423EC895.htm)). Best-effort output also carries a trust cost against Navisworks or Synchro, which are accepted in contracts **(inference)**. The project is therefore best positioned as an IFC-in assembler, checker and estimator, not an authoring replacement, consistent with the owner's stated strategy.

## "No AI inside": differentiated, but the wording needs care

Every large vendor is selling AI: Autodesk with "Trusted AI" transparency cards ([Autodesk news](https://adsknews.autodesk.com/en/news/autodesk-assistant-ai-transparency-cards-2026/)), Nemetschek with an AI strategy ([Nemetschek](https://www.nemetschek.com/en/digitalbau-2026)), and Solibri, the nearest deterministic-checking category, adding an AI assistant in beta in June 2026 ([Solibri](https://www.solibri.com/articles/solibri-release-notes-june-2026)). Autodesk's CEO is quoted aiming for "99.99 something percent" rather than 70% ([AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/features/autodesk-shows-its-ai-hand/)). Surveys name the concerns a deterministic local tool answers: 57% of 235 US contractors cite AI reliability and 54% cite data security (vendor-sponsored, small; [Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/builders-ai-transform-businesses-survey/807555/)). The same evidence shows strong AI adoption (NBS: 40%+ of UK respondents use AI; [NBS](https://www.thenbs.com/about-nbs/press-releases/ai-adoption-surges-as-digital-anxiety-grips-the-construction-sector)), so "No AI" must not read as anti-productivity **(inference)**.

Regulation rewards traceability rather than "no AI" as such. The UK golden thread requires an auditable single source of truth ([Building Safety Regulator](https://buildingsafety.campaign.gov.uk/building-safety-regulator-making-buildings-safer/building-safety-regulator-news/understanding-the-golden-thread/)), and buildingSMART's IDS aims at identical results in all checking software ([IDS](https://www.buildingsmart.org/standards/bsi-standards/information-delivery-specification-ids/)). RICS made a written AI-use standard mandatory for members from 9 March 2026 ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/rics-introduces-mandatory-ai-standard-for-surveyors-what-insurers-and-their-clients-need-to-know)). That a deterministic tool lowers a surveyor's compliance burden is an inference, true only if the runtime claim is literally true. The notes found no AEC-specific study of "No AI" messaging, no AEC product marketed that way with sales data, and no insurer statement favouring deterministic tools. Consumer evidence (Gartner: 50% prefer brands avoiding GenAI in consumer-facing content; [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260316095093/en/Gartner-Marketing-Survey-Finds-50-of-Consumers-Prefer-Brands-That-Avoid-Using-GenAI-in-Consumer-Facing-Content)) concerns consumer content, not B2B tooling. The Aerie "23% sales increase" figure is a marketing claim from a search snippet and should not be used (search-summary; [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/no-ai-disclaimers-brands-consumer-trust-2026)).

The researchers' recommended wording, which fits this project's stance, is a precise architectural statement: no AI in the runtime, same input gives same output, verifiable in the open-source repo, with AI-assisted development disclosed **(inference)**. Disclosure of AI-assisted authorship is an established open-source norm: the Linux kernel uses an "Assisted-by" tag while Gentoo and NetBSD ban AI-written code ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/software/linux/linux-distros-ban-tainted-ai-generated-code); [It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/news/linux-ai-coding-assistants-policy/)). The FTC requires substantiation of AI claims; applying that to a negative claim is inference ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/false-advertising-liability-ftc-scrutiny-and-competitive-litigation-in-the-age-of-ai-marketing/)).

## Credibility is built with open, checkable evidence

Paid buildingSMART certification is a poor first step on the evidence available. The only primary-looking cost figures are from 2020 (about EUR 25,000 to start import, EUR 27,000 for export, per [OSArch 2020](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/152/blenderbim-ifc-certification)), and a conflicting search-summary figure of EUR 1,500/2,500 could not be traced, so current fees are unverified. IfcOpenShell/Bonsai is widely used without certification, with cost cited as the reason (same thread). The cheap alternatives are real: the Validation Service is free and checks syntax, schema and implementer agreements for IFC2X3, IFC4 and IFC4X3_ADD2, files up to 256 MB ([user guide](https://buildingsmart.github.io/validate/user/index.html)); and the import-certification test criteria (15 criteria, 9 public models, Pass/Partial/Fail) are public ([import-certification.md](https://github.com/buildingSMART/Sample-Test-Files/blob/main/import-certification.md)). A self-assessed table against those files, labelled "not certified", is the notes' suggested substitute **(inference)**.

For interoperability, IDS v1.0 became a final standard on 2024-06-04 ([buildingSMART](https://www.buildingsmart.org/information-delivery-specification-ids-v1-0-is-approved-as-a-final-standard/)), and 70% of IDS-survey respondents want some IDS software certification (search-summary; [IDS survey](https://www.buildingsmart.org/ids-survey-results-2026/)). BCF exists as BCF-XML and BCF-API ([BCF-XML](https://github.com/buildingSMART/BCF-XML)). Open-source 4D exchange exists: ifc4d converts MS Project and Primavera P6 files to and from IFC ([ifc4d](https://pypi.org/project/ifc4d/)), and IfcWorkSchedule carries tasks, start/finish and float ([IFC4.3](https://ifc43-docs.standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4x3/HTML/lexical/IfcWorkSchedule.htm)). The ranking of "table stakes" (valid IFC read with no silent loss, IDS, BCF) versus differentiators (4D export, quantity round-trip) is the researchers' judgement; no source ranks them. Venues exist: the openBIM Hackathon (Porto, March 2026; [buildingSMART](https://www.buildingsmart.org/openbim-hackathon-porto-2026/)) and the openBIM Awards with a Technology category ([buildingSMART](https://www.buildingsmart.org/openbim-awards-program/)); their effect on adoption is unmeasured.

## What comparable projects teach: slow, forum-led, and funding-limited

Bonsai started as an 83-line prototype in August 2019 (search-summary; [Moult on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dion-moult-3517651a_openbim-opensource-ifcopenshell-activity-6972331582489604096-X4e0)) on top of IfcOpenShell, begun in 2011 ([opensource.construction](https://opensource.construction/projects/bonsai/)). It lives inside Blender and is funded by about **$1,300 per month** in donations from 100+ contributors plus grants such as a $15,000 Epic MegaGrant ([opensource.construction](https://opensource.construction/projects/bonsai/)); the page itself calls the project "primarily volunteer-driven". Its Open Collective shows **$96,330 raised in total** and describes a goal of moving from "a spare-time gig into a sustainable foundation" ([Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/opensourcebim)). Speckle began as a Grasshopper plugin, took a $5.5M seed, and its founder says its growth took off once it was open-sourced (search-summary; [Bricks & Bytes](https://bricks-bytes.com/technology/how-speckle-used-open-source-to-crack-aec/)). xeokit grew through embedding in OpenProject BIM 10.4 ([OpenProject](https://www.openproject.org/blog/openproject-bim-10-4/)). The accessible lesson is a working thing inside a place users already are, a forum as home base (Speckle moved from Slack to a searchable forum; [Foundamental](https://university.foundamental.com/masterclass/dimitrie-stefanescu/)), and a bigger host adopting the component. OSArch itself says public source code is proof of seriousness ([AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/collaboration/native-openbim-and-the-rise-of-open-source-in-aec/)).

The cautionary cases are about trust. xeokit's 2021 licence wording was read as not truly free until clarified ([OSArch 289](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/289/xeokit-agpl-not-quite-free-and-not-really-free-software)). IFC.js ended its bounty programme and was accused of "openwashing" after becoming a company ([OSArch 1551](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/1551/that-open-company)). Bonsai's sponsored development was reported scaled back when spending exceeded income (search-summary; [OSArch release thread](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/26/bonsai-new-release/p14)). No source shows a dated paper or buildingSMART endorsement triggering growth for any comparable project, and no time series shows a sudden "L-curve"; the researchers found only coincidences (host integration, distribution channel, grant plus forum). Stars understate infrastructure libraries, so they are a weak yardstick (IfcOpenShell 2,822 stars; [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/IfcOpenShell/IfcOpenShell)).

## What the notes could not establish

No primary 2022-2026 NBS, Dodge or Autodesk small-firm breakdown was fetched. Current buildingSMART certification fees and any open-source waiver are unknown (pages returned 403). Bentley, Nemetschek dTwin, Allplan, Trimble Connect 4D and ACC were not verified for 4D or pricing at source; most prices are third-party. No independent reviews of OpenConstructionERP, Bonsai or That Open feature depth were found. Several figures rest on search-engine summaries and are flagged above. That Open Company's revenue and headcount were not found. Chinese, Japanese and Korean markets and niche 5D tools (CostX, Vico, Kreo) were not searched.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing from "a new kind of BIM app" to "a narrow, verifiable one". The notes show that the defensible asset is not any feature, since free 4D/5D exists, but the audit story: deterministic runs, a stated tolerance, published validator results, and a limits table. Regulation and surveys point at traceability and reliability as the buyers' worries, which is a stronger argument than the badge "No AI" **(inference)**.

The slow-growth plan matches what comparable projects went through, but they also show its price: forum-led growth plateaued at donation levels near one or two part-time salaries, and trust was lost when funding or licensing changed without explanation. Plan for that, state licence and AI-assistance facts early, and treat demand from small firms as a hypothesis to test with a few named users, not a settled fact.

## Prioritized actions (at most 10)

1. **Reword the tagline to "No AI in the runtime" and publish an AI-assisted-development statement in the repo.** Evidence: ambiguity and disclosure norms ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/software/linux/linux-distros-ban-tainted-ai-generated-code)); FTC substantiation doctrine ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/false-advertising-liability-ftc-scrutiny-and-competitive-litigation-in-the-age-of-ai-marketing/)).
2. **Make "deterministic" literally testable: same input gives same output, with a published hash check, no unseeded randomness, no model calls, pinned dependencies.** Evidence: IDS identical-results ideal ([buildingSMART](https://www.buildingsmart.org/standards/bsi-standards/information-delivery-specification-ids/)); golden-thread audit trail ([BSR](https://buildingsafety.campaign.gov.uk/building-safety-regulator-making-buildings-safer/building-safety-regulator-news/understanding-the-golden-thread/)). The notes' wording recommendation is inference.
3. **Run every exported IFC through the free buildingSMART Validation Service and publish the reports.** Evidence: [user guide](https://buildingsmart.github.io/validate/user/index.html).
4. **Self-assess against the 15 public import criteria on the 9 official test files; publish a Pass/Partial/Fail table labelled "not certified".** Evidence: [import-certification.md](https://github.com/buildingSMART/Sample-Test-Files/blob/main/import-certification.md); certification cost and "poor value" views ([OSArch 2020](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/152/blenderbim-ifc-certification)). Defer paid certification.
5. **Publish a size/time/RAM table for your own buildings with a stated ceiling, and either substantiate or remove the 126K/2-3 s/95% claims.** Evidence: unverified self-claims ([BIMCompiler](https://github.com/red1oon/BIMCompiler)); peer limits ([issue #258](https://github.com/ThatOpen/engine_fragment/issues/258)).
6. **Add an import report on every load listing skipped or unsupported entities, and state the margin of error on quantities and costs.** Evidence: IFC exchange problems ([GeoBIM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.10951), search-summary); buildingSMART reports partial passes openly (import-certification.md). The "pros expect this" part is inference.
7. **Support IDS check and BCF in/out, then 4D export via IfcWorkSchedule with P6/MS Project (ifc4d prior art).** Evidence: [IDS final](https://www.buildingsmart.org/information-delivery-specification-ids-v1-0-is-approved-as-a-final-standard/); [ifc4d](https://pypi.org/project/ifc4d/). Ranking is inference.
8. **Get legal advice on "Mercedes Benz finishing" or replace it; and rewrite the differentiation claim to the surviving combination, with a "versus Bonsai / OpenConstructionERP / BEXEL" honest comparison page.** Evidence: no trademark source in the notes; competitor facts above.
9. **Post in OSArch with working demos and the evidence above, and test demand with a handful of named QS or small-contractor users before widening claims.** Evidence: [OSArch 2503](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/2503/blender-blenderbim-bonsai-is-not-on-google-search-for-best-bim-tools-for-quantity-surveyors); Speckle and Bonsai forum-led growth ([Foundamental](https://university.foundamental.com/masterclass/dimitrie-stefanescu/)). Do not copy a demand claim from the notes; none is demonstrated.
10. **State licence and funding intentions early, and plan for a plateau (grants, consulting, or a sponsor layer).** Evidence: Bonsai donation scale ([opensource.construction](https://opensource.construction/projects/bonsai/)); trust loss at xeokit and IFC.js ([OSArch 289](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/289/xeokit-agpl-not-quite-free-and-not-really-free-software); [OSArch 1551](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/1551/that-open-company)).

**Stop doing:** claiming novelty for 4D/5D; using "AI-free" or bare "No AI"; citing unverified performance numbers or the Aerie sales figure; presenting the paper date or the film as proven growth levers; buying certification now; expanding scope toward authoring parity with Revit or ArchiCAD.
