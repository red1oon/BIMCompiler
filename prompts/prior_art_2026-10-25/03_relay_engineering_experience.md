# Relays turned out smart, and history grew

**To our knowledge, no pure-relay system we could find has any reported production use for business records or money, so the engineering experience is borrowed from social, chat, notes, and budgeting software.** That experience is consistent and mostly favours your design. Every "serverless" system that grew users either added an always-on helper node (Briar Mailbox, SSB pubs and rooms, Ditto Server, Anytype sync nodes, Radicle seed nodes) or lived on relays that were not dumb: they set retention, filtered spam, went down, and lost data. The costs that persisted were not population-scale costs; they scaled with history per log (SSB's first sync took 12 hours until engineering cut it to 55 minutes) and with version skew between peers. The sharpest single-identity failure, a forked log when one SSB identity ran on two devices, is avoided by construction by one chain per device. Channel limits will bind earlier than anything else: 30 devices pushing every 5 minutes to one GitHub repo sits exactly on GitHub's recommended 6 pushes per minute (our arithmetic), and a consumer Apps Script account's 100 recipients a day is exhausted in hours by per-visit email. The research found no money-adjacent use, no mobile battery data, and no equivocation incident, so those risks are untested rather than cleared. Earlier reports on [serverless transaction precedent and deferred-query prior art](Serverless%20transaction%20systems%20precedent.md) cover authority and novelty; this one covers only operating experience.

Source depth is tagged **[page]** for a page fetched through a tool that returns a small-model digest (not a verbatim read) and **[snippet]** for a search-result summary only. Inferences are labelled as such.

## The relay was never dumb

Nostr is the only system with rigorous measurement. A 2023 study of 17.9 million posts across 712 relays found **an average of 34.6 relays holding each post, 98.2% of download traffic redundant (about 144 TiB), and 93% of posts on more than one relay** because clients fan out to compensate for unreliable retention ([arXiv 2402.05709v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05709v2)) [page]. Availability was uneven: **half of relays were up more than 99% of the time, about 20% were down more than 40% of the time, and 132 were dead**; 56% of outages were under 15 minutes and 10% over 100 minutes, with no difference between free and paid relays [page]. About 95% of free relays could not cover costs from donations [page]. An informal test (single, acknowledged-biased sample) found a median relay held 2 of 25 recent events ([nostr.ae note](https://nostr.ae/nevent1qqsrxcu78t6wqsgk60j6ntmwrdet7n38u3w2geucez62wcr6t2vt28cpqq2kd8jj?lang=en)) [snippet]. The popular strfry relay has no automatic retention policy, uses write-policy plugins to reject events, and warns that its set-reconciliation sync "can consume a lot of memory and bandwidth if the DBs are highly divergent" ([strfry README](https://github.com/hoytech/strfry)) [page]. Deletion is advisory: NIP-09 states events cannot be removed from every relay ([NIP-09](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nostr-protocol/nips/master/09.md)) [page].

Delta Chat tells the same story for email. It still allows ordinary providers but built "chatmail" relays, described as "cheap and dumb servers", because classic providers lack push and may distrust encrypted machine traffic through spam and IP-reputation systems ([Delta Chat help](https://delta.chat/en/help); [chatmail FAQ](https://chatmail.at/doc/relay/faq.html)) [page]. One user reported GMX repeatedly locking the account ([Lemmy](https://lemmy.linuxuserspace.show/post/543629)) [snippet]. Briar added an always-on Mailbox because direct delivery can be delayed "arbitrarily or indefinitely" ([Briar Mailbox README](https://code.briarproject.org/briar/briar-mailbox/-/raw/6d24f124fd48c3fa177bba171e87ce593fc26a7b/README.md)) [page]; Ditto's docs call Ditto Server "essential for non-peer-to-peer deployments" ([Ditto mesh docs](https://docs.ditto.live/key-concepts/mesh-networking.md)) [page]. Inference: your post office needs a written retention policy, writer authentication and per-key quotas, plus a cheap way to ask "what sequence numbers am I missing from device X", even if it stores only bytes.

## One identity on two devices forks the log

SSB's handbook says identities are tied to a single device; two devices mean two feeds ([SSB handbook](https://handbook.scuttlebutt.nz/faq/applications/multiple-devices)) [snippet]. The fork mechanism: an offline device misses a post made elsewhere, both then have different messages at the same sequence number, and peers reject whichever version they lack, hiding part of the feed ([Scuttlebot post](https://viewer.scuttlebot.io/%25T00Qv2Nq7L8eCnPQBxGECb08HYGn9hUMAx50aaAvCzA%3D.sha256)) [snippet]. Hypercore has one writer per core and treats key sharing as bad practice; Autobase linearises several writers' cores into an eventually consistent view ([Pear docs](https://docs.pears.com/reference/building-blocks/autobase/)) [snippet]. Delta Chat's second device is set up by QR transfer, after which "both devices are completely independent" [page]. One chain per device with a business-level key authorising device keys is where all of these converge (inference). What stayed unsolved in the literature is key recovery (SSB only had a plan: 12-word phrase, three of five friends) ([Changelog #346](https://changelog.com/podcast/346/transcript)) [page], and the danger case for you is a restored backup that re-uses sequence numbers, which looks exactly like an SSB fork.

## History growth is the cost that stays small

Staltz's October 2021 update reported initial sync falling **from about 12 hours to 55 minutes and the database from 2.0 GB to 950 MB**, mostly through a new storage and index format, and he was still "not satisfied" ([Manyverse](https://manyver.se/blog/2021-10-update)) [page]. Ink & Switch found history growth "a major performance problem" that cannot easily be truncated because a collaborator may return months later ([Local-first software](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first/)) [page]; Automerge's editor once took about 40 seconds to load because sync sent changes one by one, and the 3.0 release claims a document that had not loaded after 17 hours now loads in 9 seconds ([Towards Production](https://inkandswitch.com/automerge-rs/post/towards-production); [Automerge 3.0](https://automerge.org/blog/automerge-3/)) [snippet]. Actual Budget stores every mutation, and its documented fix for "out of sync" is a reset that clears server data and makes other devices delete and redownload; a 2022 user report describes that reset losing a budget ([Actual docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/getting-started/sync/); [issue summary](https://lightrun.com/answers/actualbudget-actual-your-data-is-still-out-of-sync)) [page / snippet]. Several redesigns (Bamboo, p2panda, Willow) added partial replication and pruning, which suggests plain append-forever logs were the part most often regretted ([Bamboo](https://github.com/AljoschaMeyer/bamboo); [p2panda](https://docs.rs/p2panda-core)) [snippet]. Inference: at tens of devices, population costs vanish but new-device bootstrap and long-offline devices do not, so signed snapshots and a retention horizon belong in the first version.

## Version skew and silent breakage generate the support load

Anytype's forum is the richest support record: "IncompatibleVersion" errors after a client update, mixed client versions blocking sync, a sync icon stuck on "syncing", and port or QUIC problems, answered by a client-side network self-test tool ([stuck syncing](https://community.anytype.io/t/sync-icon-never-stops-pulsating-and-the-status-always-stays-at-syncing-after-a-fresh-login/28910); [netcheck](https://doc.anytype.io/anytype-docs/advanced/help/troubleshooting/anysync-netcheck-tool)) [page / snippet]. Ink & Switch lists schema evolution with no authoritative schema as an open problem, and a 2023 paper calls live local schema change unsolved ([Live Local Schema Change](https://deepai.org/publication/live-local-schema-change-challenge-problems)) [snippet]. Support practice without a central log is thinly documented beyond reset and self-test tools (see gaps). Ditto's BLE limits matter only if you use a radio mesh: devices "can only handle a few concurrent connections", each BLE setup can take several seconds, and redundant links hurt in meshes of about 65 to 100 devices or more ([Ditto BLE doc](https://docs.ditto.live/sdk/latest/sync/managing-redundant-bluetooth-le-connections.md)) [page].

## The channel limits you will meet first

GitHub's recommended limits: **6 pushes per minute and 15 reads per second per repository, 5,000 branches, 10 GB repository, 1 MB per object (100 MB enforced)** ([GitHub limits](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/creating-and-managing-repositories/repository-limits)) [page, digest; the branch wording and whether each figure is advisory or enforced were not confirmed against a second source]. The REST API gives 5,000 requests an hour per user token, with secondary limits of 80 content-creating requests per minute that GitHub says can trigger "for undisclosed reasons" ([REST limits](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api)) [page]. Apps Script quotas: **100 email recipients per day consumer versus 1,500 Workspace, 20,000 mail reads/writes consumer versus 50,000, 90 versus 360 minutes of trigger runtime** ([Apps Script quotas](https://developers.google.com/apps-script/guides/services/quotas)) [page]. Android background fetch can slip "from minutes to hours" per Delta Chat, which is harmless at your visit cadence. For SQLite, the maintainers say a copy taken mid-transaction can be corrupt, the journal or WAL must travel with the file, and network-filesystem locking is unreliable; safe copies use the backup API, `VACUUM INTO`, or `sqlite3_rsync` ([SQLite: How To Corrupt](https://www.sqlite.org/howtocorrupt.html)) [page]. Syncthing does not merge: a conflict becomes a `sync-conflict` copy that then propagates everywhere ([Syncthing forum](https://forum.syncthing.net/t/how-does-conflict-resolution-work/15113)) [snippet]; Dropbox-with-SQLite reports are anecdotes of conflicted copies ([Xojo](https://forum.xojo.com/t/sqlite-database-on-dropbox/32735)) [snippet].

## How each lesson maps to your design

Status is judged only from your one-paragraph description of the design, not from its specification; "covered by design" means the structure implies it, but nothing we read shows a test that proves it.

| Lesson | Evidence | Status | What to witness or add (inference) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relays are not dumb (retention, auth, quotas) | Nostr 34.6x replication, outages; strfry; chatmail | Not covered | Written retention policy, per-device write credential, quota behaviour; second mirror for the one real post office |
| Pure P2P added an always-on helper | Briar, SSB, Ditto, Anytype | Already covered | Post office is the helper; plan its own backup (Actual's operator-backup note) |
| Multi-device on one identity forks | SSB fork | Covered by design, not witnessed | Witness: restore-from-backup and cloned device must be detected, not silently accepted |
| History growth / initial sync | SSB 12 h to 55 min; Automerge 40 s; Actual | Not covered | Signed checkpoints, new-device bootstrap time budget, horizon for long-offline devices |
| Version skew / schema evolution | Anytype errors; Ink & Switch | Not covered | Per-event schema version, upcasters, a gate that refuses to apply unknown versions |
| Debugging without a central log | Actual reset; Anytype netcheck | Not covered | Exportable diagnostic bundle (chain heads, last-seen sequence per peer, versions); owner-device authoritative restore with pre-reset backup |
| Git push and API limits | GitHub 6 pushes/min advisory | Covered by design, not witnessed | Burst test with 30+ devices; batch pushes; `ls-remote` before fetch |
| Email/Gmail quotas and deliverability | Apps Script quotas; chatmail; GMX anecdote | Not covered | Batch per visit; treat a consumer mailbox as demo-only (inference from quotas) |
| SQLite never shared as a file | SQLite docs; Syncthing; Dropbox | Already covered | Per-device DB plus event shipping; assert no code path syncs the `.db` |
| Corrections as new events, deletion advisory | NIP-09 | Covered by design, not witnessed | Witness a compensating entry applying on all devices |

## What the research did not find

No production business, accounting, lending, or payments deployment of Nostr, SSB, Hypercore, Earthstar/Willow, p2panda, Radicle, Briar, Matrix P2P, or Anytype was found; Nostr's money use is tipping. No mobile battery or bandwidth measurements exist in anything reached. No equivocation or fork incident beyond SSB's early community posts was found, and no Nostr key-loss reports were retrieved. Ditto publishes customers (Delta, Japan Airlines, Lufthansa) but no device counts, latencies, or failure postmortems; the Delta numbers rest on an unreadable vendor PDF. Matrix P2P's "hiatus until funding" is a secondhand quote about money, not a technical failure ([Lemmy quote](https://lemmy.linuxuserspace.show/comment/1694920)) [snippet]. Not reached at all: Jami, Berty, git-annex, Obsidian and Logseq sync reports, Tailscale DERP, Hacker News threads, GitHub issue trackers, Gmail's own send limits, and any Willow "pause" (unconfirmed). Source quality is uneven: the only independent measurement is the Nostr paper, the SSB numbers are maintainer newsletters, Syncthing, Dropbox, KeePass and Briar user experience is forum anecdote, and many entries are snippets. Every fetched figure came from a digest, not a verbatim read, so cite them to the primary page before relying on them in a specification.

## Conclusion

The evidence converts your design's open questions from "will the post office work?" to "which duties does it quietly acquire?". Retention, writer authentication, mirrors, and checkpoints are the duties that every comparable system ended up with, and none of the sources shows a way to avoid them. The part that is genuinely yours to prove is not on the transport: it is the restore-versus-fork case, version-skew gating, and a recovery path that does not repeat Actual's reset losing data. Those three can be witnessed on your own chains without any outside evidence, which is where the research's silence about business use leaves you.
