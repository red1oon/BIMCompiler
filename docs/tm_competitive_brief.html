The honest verdict

A top contender in one specific segment — browser-native 4D that generates a schedule from the model — not a drop-in Bentley SYNCHRO replacement.

The incumbent workflow assumes a planner already built a P6 schedule; ours derives a defensible one from geometry, in a browser, folded to live ERP cost. Against the tool 55% of the market actually uses — Navisworks TimeLiner, whose own docs call its Gantt “read-only” — the Time Machine is ahead today on generation, CPM, cost and delivery. It loses a head-to-head only where the ask is a contractor’s existing P6 schedule with resource levelling and a decade of claims-grade track record.

Where it wins

Four positions the research confirms are genuinely ours

Generation from geometry

Differentiator · uncontested

We derive the build sequence from the model — Z-order, structural support gating, crew caps.

A Frontiers survey states doing this automatically “is not currently possible” commercially. Bexel and ALICE generate from rule/recipe templates, not the geometry’s own support graph.

Field: ~80% of the market (Navisworks, SYNCHRO) generates nothing — they map an imported schedule.

Schedule interop

Stronger than assumed

Three parsers — XER, P6 PMXML, MSPDI — plus MSPDI export, with a re-export-safe bind token instead of brittle GUID mapping.

Navisworks (55% share) imports P6/MSP but exports back only to CSV or MS Project XML, per Autodesk’s own docs. Round-trip depth across the desktop suites varies and is worth confirming case by case.

Gap: no XER/PMXML writer yet — the single piece between us and documented Bexel parity.

Native CPM

Verified in code

Exact critical-path over the authored DAG — FS/SS/FF/SF with lag, float and critical flags written to tasks.

Navisworks has no CPM engine — Autodesk calls its Gantt “read-only.” Only SYNCHRO and Bexel match us here.

Gap: resource levelling — and in our code that’s a deliberate refuse, not a missing feature.

Cost / EVM / ERP

Rare depth

Folds PP_Order, earned-value and variance from a live ERP into the 4D view.

Named earned-value in a 4D tool is rare — SYNCHRO Perform and Bexel are the clearest examples, and in SYNCHRO’s case across separate products. Navisworks records cost as manual columns, not an EVM engine.

Ahead of: everyone whose “5D” is cost-loaded tasks with no earned value.

Where it loses — and the plan

One open front left; the scale ceiling shipped
01

Close the interop gap — add an XER / PMXML writer

We import P6 and export MS Project, but a P6 shop can’t reingest our output natively. The fix is a serializer mirroring parsers we already own — verified by round-tripping through our own reader. Lands us at documented parity with Bexel, ahead of the unverified rest.

02

✅ Scale ceiling wired — box-proxy shipped into Time Machine (2026-07-20)

Box the inactive set, keep per-element mesh only for what's under construction AND actually in view (camera-distance + frustum, the field's own LOD0/LOD2 convention). The field's core dilemma is “merge or 4D”; this threads it. Live on LTU (122k elements) in production: real construction-window geometry stays full LOD400, everything already-built but out of view collapses to a lightweight wireframe box — verified on real hardware, not just headless. What's still unproven: millions of elements (only tested to 122k), and no formal LTU-vs-HHS side-by-side capture was produced — the verification here was live user testing on the real target building, which is what caught and fixed a design gap (time-based vs. view-based swap) a synthetic capture likely wouldn't have surfaced.

Methodology & limitations

How to read this — and where the evidence is thin
Our scores

Drawn directly from our own source code and stand behind. Scale is our sharpest edge and our honest limit at once: a 125k-element model now scrubs smoothly in a browser and plays back its full 4D construction sequence with a DLOD box-proxy (shipped 2026-07-20) — where the desktop incumbents live and browser rivals cap out — but millions is not yet proven; that's the next honest limit, not this one.

Competitor claims

Compiled from public vendor documentation and secondary sources as of July 2026. Where a capability rests on vendor marketing rather than primary documentation, we treat it as stated but not independently confirmed rather than asserting it either way.

Round-trip depth

P6/MS-Project round-trip fidelity varies across the desktop suites and, in several cases, could not be confirmed from current primary docs within this research. Any head-to-head on interop should be validated tool-by-tool with a live licence.

Market movement

The category moves — Trimble Vico Office reached end-of-life in June 2024, and Autodesk’s construction line has been rebranding — so any comparison is a snapshot, not a standing fact.

Scale figures

Vendor element-count ceilings are rarely published; the real-world limits circulating in forums are the least-sourced numbers in the space and are worth re-checking before being quoted as fact.
